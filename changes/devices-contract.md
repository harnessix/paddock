- New: the device-facing contract. A device program is an `exec` command (the request on its standard input, the response on its standard output, exit 65 rejected, 75 still waiting, 77 declined) or a `serve` program that answers HTTP/1.1 on the Unix socket `$PADDOCK_DEVICE_SOCKET` (`POST /v1/call` with the `Paddock-*` headers of the caller, `GET /v1/health`, `GET /v1/catalog`, `Paddock-Cost`, `Paddock-Protocol-Min`). `paddock help device-guide` has the tables.
- New: `paddock device-serve` (hidden), the supervisor that puts a program behind such a socket: it forks an `exec` program per request in an empty working folder with the caller in `PADDOCK_*` variables, kills the process group at `timeout` and on cancel, caps the output at `max_response`, and proxies to a `serve` program after its health answered, with a restart backoff of 1, 2 and 4 seconds and `failed` after three exits within a minute. `--connect PORT:SOCKET` relays `127.0.0.1:PORT` to a socket of the broker. The log has one line per request and the program's stderr, never a body, 10 MiB with one rotation. It ends with its parent and on SIGTERM.
- New: one client in the broker speaks to every device program (`devices::program`), with the status mapping of the contract, chunked bodies, the `Paddock-Cost` header and two enforcers of `timeout` and `max_response`. `devices::outproc::OutOfProcess` runs the gate, the budget, the `concurrency` slots and the audit for such a device; the audit line has `shape`, `instance` and `http`. The device sandbox and the host mode plug into it.
- Changed: the example script of `paddock device example` turns off the request log of Python's HTTP handler, which raised on a Unix socket.
- Fix: audit lines of concurrent device requests cannot interleave anymore (one write per line).
- Fix: the catalog reason of a `shape = "serve"` table names what it needs (a device sandbox or `run = "host"`), not an internal work item.
